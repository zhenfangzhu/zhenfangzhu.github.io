A plane makes an emergency landing on a downtown pedestrian street, passing through a surreal sense of unreality.

I was on a plane heading somewhere when a male flight attendant and a mechanic asked several of us to change seats. I nearly became angry and demanded a reason. He looked at me without speaking, tears in his eyes, and the cabin had gone still. Then I understood.

Trying not to alarm the other passengers, I quietly asked whether something had gone wrong with the plane. He did not deny it. I silently took the window seat they had found for me. A middle-aged crew member sat nearby, forcing himself to look calm. There seemed to be only a small chance of success, and the proposed landing site was somewhere in the city. Nothing I could do would change it, so I took out my phone and tried to send my parents what might be a final message.

No reply came. The plane tilted sharply downward. I prepared to record a video, surrounded by a fear too large to imagine. I wondered whether I had already died—whether I was in heaven or another timeline. The middle-aged person beside me kept their mouth closed. I could not tell whether they were waiting to die or waiting for judgment.

With an immense roar, the plane touched down and slid for a long time. We finally stopped on the broad road of a pedestrian street. There was no fire and the fuselage remained intact. The cabin door opened and rescuers helped us down.

I descended a bright ladder and called home, saying that the landing had succeeded and that I might be on the news. Inside the plane I had thought about the future and about whether this body was even mine, or whether the whole thing belonged to another layer of the dream.

Walking through the food street, I saw a woman dressed as if for swimming or bathing. Everything was busy and yet strangely quiet. People sat eating as though nothing had happened. The world outside had not looked like this before the accident. Where, exactly, was I?
